### What happens

The migrations of yarn.lock and pnpm-lock.yaml end with `Lockfile::fetch_necessary_package_metadata_after_yarn_or_pnpm_migration(&mut self, manager)` (`src/install/lockfile.rs:1485`, called from `yarn.rs:1920` and `pnpm.rs:1623`). Its first step is `populate_manifest_cache(manager, Packages::All)`. For `Packages::All` that function walks the packages of `manager.lockfile`, through a shared reference that it makes from a raw pointer to the manager (`src/install/PackageManager/PopulateManifestCache.rs:144`).

At the call sites that load the lockfile in place, `manager.lockfile` is the same allocation as `self`:

- `install_with_manager` (`src/install/PackageManager/install_with_manager.rs:86`)
- `PackageManager::load_lockfile_from_cwd` (`src/install/PackageManager.rs:838`)
- `bun pm migrate` (`src/runtime/cli/package_manager_command.rs:733`)
- `bun outdated` (`outdated_command.rs:116`) and `bun pm scan` (`scan_command.rs:46`)

So the migration reads the lockfile through a `&Lockfile` while the `&mut Lockfile` that it got from the caller is live. I know of no wrong result from this.

Two more facts, from a read of the code:

- The comments at those call sites said that the load "never re-projects `manager.lockfile`". #44294 removes that sentence. It does not change the code.
- `bun why` (`why_command.rs:351`) and `bun pm diff` (`pm_diff_command.rs:387`) take the lockfile out of the manager before the load. `populate_manifest_cache` then sees an empty lockfile and requests no manifest. The step that fills `bin`, `os` and `cpu` after it has only the manifests of the disk cache.

The line numbers are those of main at 2722608f47.

### Possible change

Give `populate_manifest_cache` the lockfile (or the slices that it reads) as an argument, and do not read `manager.lockfile` there. The migration then passes `self`, and the two kinds of caller behave the same.
